What the numbers say

  1. Use a contract manufacturer, not your own factory. Your own line costs about $790k to set up and about $49,000 a month to run. It only pays off above roughly 1.3 to 2.4 million cartons a year, which is close to everything a small line can make.
  2. Ingredients are the small part. They are about $0.42 of the $1.40 first-run cost. The manufacturer's fee and the packaging make up most of the rest. Removing xylitol saves only about 5 cents a carton.
  3. Run size is the biggest lever. A 20,000-carton run costs about $1.40 a carton. At 100,000 cartons and bulk ingredient prices it falls to about $0.71.
  4. Sell the first run directly. At $2.99, you make $1.41 a carton selling in person, $0.40 selling to local stores and $0.37 on online 6-packs. Through a distributor you would lose $0.24 a carton until costs come down.
  5. Break-even takes volume. Year one roughly breaks even on running costs at about 22,500 cartons sold directly. A regional retail business breaks even at about 124,000 cartons a year.

What you could charge

Mainstream gum sells for $1.39 to $1.79 a pack. Premium and natural gums sell for $2.49 to $2.99, and functional gums for about $3.99. A whitening gum with a message in every wrapper fits the premium tier: $2.49 to $3.49.

What you need to charge

To break even selling to local stores, the shelf price must be at least $2.33 on the first run and $1.19 at scale. Through a distributor it must be at least $3.61 on the first run and $1.84 at scale.

How to read this study. Every figure is a planning estimate built from public price lists, supplier listings and market reports gathered in November 2026. None is a quote. Gum manufacturers do not publish prices, so their fees are the least certain numbers here. Page 6 shows how reliable each input is and what to ask for when you request quotes.

Where the money goes

About a third is the first production run, which you earn back as you sell it. The whitening study is the largest single test. It is only needed to print "Whitening" on the box, so it can wait until demand is proven.

A cheaper first step

Some private-label gum makers will put their existing sugar-free gum in your packaging. Listings show $1.80 to $4.00 a box with minimum orders of 300 to 1,000 boxes. A 1,000-box market test could cost a few thousand dollars and show whether people want the message idea before you pay for a custom formula. These are unverified overseas listings, and imported food must still meet FDA rules.

Foil-lined wrappers

Listings price printed foil-and-paper gum wrap at $1.80 to $6.70 per kg, which works out to about 1 to 3 cents a carton. The material is cheap. The hurdles are the minimum order of 300 to 500 kg, enough for about 100,000 cartons, and printing plates at $200 to $800 per color.

Plain paper, message inside

Plain paper likely saves a cent or two a carton at most. Its real advantage is flexibility: smaller minimum orders, and digital printing with no plates, so the set of messages can be large and can change often. Add one sealed inner pack per carton to keep the gum fresh.

Electricity is minor: roughly 0.5 to 1 kWh per kg of gum at about 13 cents per kWh, well under 1 cent a carton. People and rent are the real running costs.

When would your own factory pay off?

Running it costs about $588,000 a year whether or not you sell anything. What it saves is the manufacturer's fee of $0.25 to $0.45 a carton. So it needs 1.3 to 2.4 million cartons a year just to cover running costs, before repaying the set-up money. A small line with one wrapping machine makes about 2.4 million cartons a year running flat out. A bench-top kit for testing recipes yourself is a different matter: a small mixer, hand roller and tools cost about $10,000 to $30,000.

Ask every manufacturer for

  • Price per carton at three volumes: their minimum, 50,000 and 250,000
  • Minimum order in cartons, per flavor
  • Development fee, what it covers, and whether it is credited to the first order
  • Who owns the formula
  • Wrapper options and their minimum orders: foil-lined, and plain paper printed inside
  • Whether they supply shelf-life and stain-removal data
