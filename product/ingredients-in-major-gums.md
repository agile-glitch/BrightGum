# What's in the major gum brands

A plain-language guide to the ingredients on mainstream gum labels: what each
one does, which brands use it, where the safety and regulatory questions are,
and how bright's recipes compare.

> **How reliable this is.** The ingredient lists were gathered in November 2026
> from retailer listings, read through search-result excerpts. They were not
> checked against physical packs, and manufacturers reformulate. Lists marked
> *partial* were cut off or paraphrased. Before quoting any brand's ingredients
> publicly, check a current pack.

---

## 1. The standard recipe

Nearly every mainstream sugar-free gum is built the same way. Extra Spearmint is
the template:

> Sorbitol, Gum Base, Glycerol, Natural and Artificial Flavors; Less than 2% of:
> Hydrogenated Starch Hydrolysate, Aspartame, Mannitol, Acesulfame K, Soy
> Lecithin, BHT (to Maintain Freshness), Colors (Turmeric, Blue 1 Lake).

5 gum is almost identical, and Orbit and Trident differ only in details. Read as
a formula, it has seven jobs:

| Job | Ingredient in the standard recipe | Roughly how much |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk and sweetness | Sorbitol (plus mannitol, hydrogenated starch hydrolysate) | Over half |
| The chew | Gum base | About a quarter to a third |
| Softness | Glycerol (glycerin) | A few percent |
| Flavor | Natural and artificial flavors | 1–3% |
| Lasting sweetness | Aspartame + acesulfame K | Under 1% |
| Mixing and texture | Soy lecithin | Under 1% |
| Shelf life and looks | BHT, colors | Trace |

Sugar gums (Doublemint, Juicy Fruit, Big Red, bubble gums) swap the sorbitol for
**sugar, dextrose and corn syrup** and keep everything else, including the
aspartame and acesulfame K for lasting sweetness.

## 2. Ingredient by ingredient

### Sweeteners that make up the bulk

| Ingredient | What it is | Who uses it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sorbitol** | Sugar alcohol made from corn glucose. About 60% as sweet as sugar. | First ingredient in Extra, Orbit, Trident, 5, Eclipse | The cheapest bulk sweetener, and the industry standard. A laxative warning is required only at very high intake (50 g a day). |
| **Xylitol** | Sugar alcohol from corn cobs or birch. As sweet as sugar, with a cooling taste. Mouth bacteria can't ferment it. | First ingredient in Orbit White, Ice Breakers Ice Cubes, Mentos Pure Fresh, PUR, Spry, Epic. Present in small amounts in Trident and Orbit. | Many mainstream gums that mention xylitol have under 2% of it. Toxic to dogs. See the note on a 2024 heart study in section 4. |
| **Mannitol** | Sugar alcohol. Doesn't absorb moisture. | Most mainstream gums, in small amounts | Keeps sticks from getting sticky; also used as dusting powder. |
| **Maltitol / maltitol syrup** | Sugar alcohol from corn starch. | Trident White, Eclipse, Ice Breakers, Mentos | Used in pellet gums and coatings. |
| **Hydrogenated starch hydrolysate** | A syrup of mixed sugar alcohols. | Extra, Orbit, 5 | Binder and softener; keeps the stick from drying out. |
| **Sugar, dextrose, corn syrup, glucose syrup** | Sugars. | Doublemint, Juicy Fruit, Big Red, Hubba Bubba, Dubble Bubble, Bazooka | Feed the bacteria that cause cavities. |

### High-intensity sweeteners

These are hundreds of times sweeter than sugar, so tiny amounts keep the gum
tasting sweet after the bulk sweetener has dissolved. This is what makes
mainstream gum seem long-lasting.

| Ingredient | Who uses it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspartame** | Almost every mainstream gum, sugar-free and sugared | Requires the label line "Phenylketonurics: contains phenylalanine." In 2023 a WHO agency classed it as "possibly carcinogenic" on limited evidence, while the WHO/FAO expert committee kept its acceptable daily intake unchanged. Legal everywhere, but it's the sweetener shoppers most often try to avoid. |
| **Acesulfame potassium (Ace-K)** | Almost every mainstream gum | Usually paired with aspartame. Europe's food safety agency raised its acceptable daily intake in 2025. |
| **Sucralose** | Trident, Ice Breakers, Mentos, caffeinated gums | Europe's food safety agency kept its acceptable daily intake unchanged in 2026. |
| **Neotame** | Ice Breakers Ice Cubes | A newer relative of aspartame, used in very small amounts. |

### Gum base

"Gum base" is one word on the label for a blend the maker doesn't have to
itemize. US rules (21 CFR 172.615) allow it to include:

| Part of the base | Typical substances | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Elastomers (the rubber) | Synthetic: polyisobutylene, butyl rubber, styrene-butadiene rubber. Natural: chicle and other tree latexes. | Bounce and stretch |
| Resins | Polyvinyl acetate; glycerol esters of wood or gum rosin; terpene resins | Body and chew |
| Waxes and softeners | Paraffin and microcrystalline wax, polyethylene, vegetable oils, rice bran wax | Softness |
| Fillers | Calcium carbonate, talc | Bulk and texture |
| Antioxidant | BHT, or tocopherols (vitamin E) | Stops the base going rancid |

Modern mainstream gum base is mostly **synthetic polymers**: food-grade
relatives of materials used in adhesives and inner tubes. That is legal and
long-established, but it surprises people. **Simply Gum and Glee** market against
it with a natural **chicle** base (tree sap) and list what's in it.

*(The synthetic names above are from the regulation as I know it; the research
could only confirm the natural latexes and rosin esters. Check ecfr.gov.)*

### Softeners, emulsifiers and coatings

| Ingredient | What it does | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Glycerol / glycerin** | Keeps gum soft and flexible | Nearly all |
| **Soy lecithin** | Helps ingredients mix; softens | Nearly all mainstream gums. A soy allergen. Spry uses sunflower lecithin instead. |
| **Acacia (gum arabic)** | Binder for the hard shell on pellet gum; carrier for flavors | Orbit White, Trident White, Eclipse, Ice Breakers, PUR, Spry |
| **Carnauba wax, candelilla wax, beeswax** | Shine on pellet gum | Orbit White, Eclipse, Trident White, PUR, Spry, Glee |
| **Gelatin** | Texture | Ice Breakers Ice Cubes. Not vegetarian. |
| **Corn starch, rice flour** | Dusting powder so pieces don't stick | Bubble gums, Simply Gum |

### Preservative

| Ingredient | What it does | Who uses it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BHT** (butylated hydroxytoluene) | Antioxidant: stops the gum base going stale | Every mainstream gum found, sugar-free and sugared | In May 2026 the FDA opened a formal safety reassessment of BHT. There is no ban. PUR uses **tocopherols** (vitamin E) instead. |

### Colors

| Ingredient | Who uses it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue 1 / Blue 1 Lake** | Extra, 5, Orbit White, Big Red, Trident | Petroleum-based dye. In April 2025 the FDA asked makers to drop these dyes voluntarily by the end of 2026. |
| **Red 40 / Red 40 Lake** | Big Red, Hubba Bubba, Dubble Bubble, Bazooka | Same voluntary phase-out. |
| **Red 3** | Dubble Bubble | FDA revoked its approval in January 2025; food makers have until 2027. |
| **Titanium dioxide** | Trident White; one listing of Epic | A white pigment. Banned in food in the EU since 2022; still legal in the US, with a petition to ban it pending. |
| **Turmeric, chlorophyll** | Extra (turmeric), Glee (chlorophyll) | Plant-based colors. |

### Whitening ingredients

| Brand | What does the whitening | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Orbit White** | Sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) | Mild polish |
| **Trident White** | Sodium stearate | A soap-like ingredient that helps lift surface stains. The white look of the gum itself comes from titanium dioxide. An older list also shows a milk-derived mineral complex (Recaldent). |
| **Spry, Epic** | Calcium carbonate | Mild polish, also a filler |

No mainstream gum uses peroxide.

### Caffeine

| Brand | Caffeine per piece | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Military Energy Gum | 100 mg | Sugar-based gum |
| Run Gum | 50 mg | Sugar-free; also adds taurine and B vitamins |
| bright (planned) | 40 mg | Below both |

## 4. Ingredients under scrutiny

| Ingredient | What's happening | In bright? |
|---|---|---|
| **Petroleum-based dyes** (Blue 1, Red 40 and others) | FDA asked makers in April 2025 to remove them voluntarily by the end of 2026. Mars announced dye-free versions of some products for 2026. | No. bright uses no colors. |
| **Red 3** | US approval revoked January 2025. | No |
| **Titanium dioxide** | Banned in EU food since 2022; US petition pending; some state label laws. | No |
| **BHT** | FDA safety reassessment opened May 2026. No ban. | **Possibly, inside the gum base.** See section 5. |
| **Aspartame** | "Possibly carcinogenic" classification in 2023 on limited evidence; intake limits unchanged. Needs a phenylalanine warning. | No. bright uses sucralose. |
| **Xylitol** | A 2024 study in the *European Heart Journal* linked high blood levels of xylitol to more heart attacks and strokes. It was observational, in a high-risk group, and doesn't prove cause. Regulators haven't acted. | **Yes: bright's main ingredient in the xylitol versions.** See section 5. |
| **Sucralose, Ace-K** | Recently re-reviewed in Europe; both kept or raised their safe intake levels. | Yes (sucralose in all; Ace-K in the long-lasting and no-xylitol recipes) |
| **Soy lecithin** | A major allergen that must be declared. | No. bright uses sunflower lecithin. |

## 5. How bright compares

| | Mainstream sugar-free (Extra, Orbit, Trident, 5) | Xylitol brands (PUR, Spry, Epic) | bright (standard xylitol recipe) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main sweetener | Sorbitol | Xylitol | Xylitol (40%), then sorbitol |
| Lasting sweetener | Aspartame + Ace-K | None | Sucralose |
| Phenylalanine warning | Yes | No | No |
| Artificial colors | Usually | No | No |
| Titanium dioxide | Trident White | One Epic listing | No |
| BHT on the label | Yes | No (PUR uses vitamin E) | Not listed yet; see below |
| Soy | Yes | Varies | No |
| Whitening ingredient | Baking soda or sodium stearate (whitening lines only) | Calcium carbonate | Calcium carbonate + sodium tripolyphosphate |
| A message in the wrapper | No | No | Yes |

### What this means for bright

1. **bright's label is already cleaner than the mainstream brands'.** No
   aspartame, no dyes, no titanium dioxide, no soy. Those are the four
   ingredients drawing the most regulatory and shopper attention, so
   "no aspartame, no dyes" is a claim worth considering for the box, once the
   final recipe confirms it.
2. **Ask the gum base supplier about BHT.** Every mainstream brand lists BHT
   because it's in their gum base. bright's recipes don't list it, but a
   standard gum base usually contains it, and then it has to appear on the
   label. To keep it off, ask for a base preserved with **tocopherols**
   (vitamin E), as PUR does.
3. **The gum base is the least "clean" part of any gum,** bright included. If a
   natural positioning matters later, a chicle-based or "plastic-free" base is
   the route Simply Gum and Glee took. It costs more and chews differently.
4. **Sodium tripolyphosphate is unusual.** None of the gums found here list it;
   the whitening lines use baking soda, sodium stearate or calcium carbonate.
   It's a recognized stain-lifting ingredient in toothpaste and some gums, but
   ask the manufacturer what their whitening bases use, and consider
   **baking soda plus calcium carbonate** as a simpler, more familiar pairing.
5. **Keep the xylitol heart study on your radar.** One observational study
   doesn't establish harm, and xylitol's dental benefit is well supported. But
   bright's xylitol recipes put 1 g in every stick as the headline feature. This
   is one more reason the no-xylitol version is worth keeping ready.
6. **Mainstream gum lasts because of aspartame and Ace-K,** the lasting
   sweeteners. bright's long-lasting recipes do the same job with encapsulated
   sucralose and Ace-K, without aspartame.
