# bright gum: no-xylitol formula sheets

A separate version of every recipe **without xylitol**, for the case where a
xylitol gum proves too hard or too expensive to get right. The xylitol recipes
stay in [`formula-sheets.md`](formula-sheets.md) and
[`formula-sheets-long-lasting.md`](formula-sheets-long-lasting.md); nothing there
is replaced. The matching boxes are in `packaging/no-xylitol/`.

> **Status: untested.** Like the other sheets, these are development formulas on
> paper. A manufacturer will fine-tune them, and flavor duration has to be
> measured with the timed chew test in `formula-sheets-long-lasting.md`.

## What this version is

This is the **standard recipe type for sugar-free stick gum**: sorbitol as the
main sweetener instead of xylitol. Most sugar-free gum on store shelves is made
this way, so nearly every gum manufacturer has a proven base to start from.

It keeps everything else that makes bright what it is: the whitening
ingredients, no dyes, no major allergens, six flavors, and a positive message in
every wrapper. It's also built **long-lasting from the start**, with the same
two-stage flavor and slow-release sweeteners as the long-lasting xylitol sheets.

## What changes, honestly

| | Xylitol versions | No-xylitol version |
|---|---|---|
| Main sweetener | Xylitol (40%) | Sorbitol (about 52%) |
| Ingredient cost | Higher | About 5 cents less per carton than the long-lasting xylitol recipe, and about the same as the standard one (see `finance/`) |
| Ease of manufacturing | Harder: high-xylitol stick gum can turn hard and brittle over time, and fewer manufacturers make it | Easier: the industry-standard recipe, with stock bases available |
| Flavor duration | Depends on the slow-release flavor and sweeteners | The same: depends on the slow-release flavor and sweeteners |
| Dental story | Strong: 1 g of xylitol per stick | Weaker: still sugar-free, but no xylitol benefit |
| Who it appeals to | Shoppers who look for xylitol (a small group) plus everyone else | Everyone who buys sugar-free gum |
| Box wording | "Xylitol" on front and back | "A message in every wrapper" takes its place |

**One thing to be clear about:** removing xylitol does not, by itself, make the
flavor last longer. Sorbitol also dissolves in the first few minutes of chewing.
What makes flavor last is the slow-release flavor and sweeteners, which work with
or without xylitol. The real gains from dropping xylitol are a simpler recipe to
manufacture and a more stable stick. The ingredient saving is small.

**A middle option:** keep a smaller amount of xylitol (for example 10–15%) for
its clean, cool sweetness, list it in the ingredients, and leave it off the
front of the box. Ask the manufacturer to quote this too.

## Claims on the no-xylitol box

- **"Sugar-Free"** still applies.
- **"Whitening"** still needs stain-removal test data, as before.
- **"Long-lasting"** can't be printed until a timed chew test backs it up.
- **"Xylitol"** is removed from the front, the back heading and the back line.
  In their place the box says **"A message in every wrapper,"** which is the one
  thing no other gum on the shelf offers.
