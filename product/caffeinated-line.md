# bright gum: caffeinated line (dark boxes)

**Deep-blue boxes mean caffeinated.** The caffeinated line uses an inverted box: a
deep-blue background with the white bright wordmark, so it can't be mistaken
for the regular white boxes on a shelf.

First flavor: **Cotton Candy**.

manufacturer about coated (encapsulated) caffeine. It releases more slowly and
tastes milder, but its coating may add an ingredient to the label.

paper with the message printed on the inside, plus a sealed inner pack. See
"Wrapper options" in `formula-sheets.md`.

## Caffeine: what to know before launching

**1. The flavor and the caffeine together are the biggest risk.** In 2013 the
FDA announced it would look into caffeine added to foods, especially products
that appeal to children, after a major gum maker launched a caffeinated gum. That
company paused its product. A **cotton candy** flavor reads as a kids' flavor, so
a caffeinated cotton candy gum is exactly the combination regulators worry about.

Ways to lower that risk:
- Keep the dark box clearly adult: "Caffeinated" on the front, the caffeine
  amount and the advisory on the back (the mockups already do this).
- Market it to adults only: college students, gyms, people studying or working
  late. No cartoon characters, no kid-focused ads, no sampling to minors.
- Consider a less childlike name for the caffeinated version, or launch the
  caffeinated line with a mint flavor first and keep Cotton Candy caffeine-free.

**2. Caffeine's FDA status in gum needs checking.** Caffeine is listed as
generally recognized as safe only for cola-type drinks at up to 0.02%
(21 CFR 182.1180). Its use in gum relies on the manufacturer's own safety
assessment. Ask your manufacturer for their documentation, and have your label
reviewer confirm it.

**3. What's on the box now (and why):**
- **Caffeine in the ingredient list.** Required.
- **"40 mg caffeine per piece."** Voluntary, but FDA encourages it, and buyers expect it.
- **Advisory:** "Not recommended for children, people who are pregnant or nursing,
  or anyone sensitive to caffeine. Limit 5 pieces a day."
  Five pieces is 200 mg, half of the 400 mg a day the FDA cites as a level not
  generally linked to negative effects in healthy adults.

**4. Don't make energy or performance claims** like "boosts focus" or "more
energy" without evidence. "Caffeinated · 40 mg" states a fact; "energy gum"
starts to sound like a benefit claim.

**5. Extra tests for this flavor:** caffeine content per piece (lab assay, to
prove the 40 mg on the box) and caffeine stability over shelf life.
