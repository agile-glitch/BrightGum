# bright gum: draft formula

A starting formula to send to a **contract gum manufacturer** ("co-packer").
Gum is made on specialized mixing, extruding and rolling lines, and almost every
new gum brand starts this way: you own the brand, the recipe targets and the
packaging, and they make it.

> **Updated:** the finished v1 recipes for all three flavors (exact grams, batch
> quantities, label ingredient lines and nutrition) are in
> [`formula-sheets.md`](formula-sheets.md). Where the two differ, the sheets win:
> they drop all colors and use sunflower lecithin instead of soy.

**These percentages are a development starting point, not a finished recipe.**
The co-packer will adjust them on their equipment, run shelf-life tests and give
you the official ingredient list and Nutrition Facts.

---

## 1. The product

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | Stick gum, **15 sticks per carton** (your 3.00 × 2.40 in carton) |
| Stick weight | ~2.7 g (typical for a slab/stick) |
| Sugar | **Sugar-free** (under 0.5 g sugar per stick) |
| Benefit 1: Xylitol | **≥ 1.0 g xylitol per stick**, listed as the first ingredient |
| Benefit 2: Whitening | Stain-lifting polish (no peroxide) |
| Flavors | Peppermint (lead), Blue Raspberry, Pickle |

## 2. Why these two benefits work

**Xylitol.** Mouth bacteria can't ferment xylitol into acid the way they do
sugar. Chewing any sugar-free gum also boosts saliva, which rinses and neutralizes
acid. Studies that show a dental benefit use about **5–10 g of xylitol a day,
over 3+ chews**. At 1 g per stick, that's 5–6 sticks a day. That's why the
"≥ 1 g per stick" target matters: lots of gums contain *some* xylitol, but not
enough to count.

**Whitening.** Gum can't change the color of the tooth itself (that takes
peroxide). It *can* scrub off and block **surface stains** from coffee, tea and
soda. That's the honest promise, and it's the one you can legally back up
(see `regulations/regulations.md`).

## 3. Draft formula: Peppermint (base recipe)

Per 2.7 g stick.

| Ingredient | Role | % | g / stick |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Gum base | The chewy part (food-grade polymers, resins, softeners) | 30.0 | 0.81 |
| **Xylitol** | Main sweetener; dental benefit | **40.0** | **1.08** |
| Sorbitol (powder) | Bulk sweetener; keeps texture soft | 18.5 | 0.50 |
| Calcium carbonate | Gentle polish (whitening); filler | 4.0 | 0.11 |
| **Sodium tripolyphosphate** | Loosens stains, stops new ones sticking (whitening) | **1.5** | 0.04 |
| Glycerin | Keeps the stick flexible, not crumbly | 3.0 | 0.08 |
| Peppermint flavor (peppermint oil + menthol) | Flavor and cooling | 2.5 | 0.07 |
| Sunflower lecithin | Helps mix and soften (allergen-free) | 0.3 | 0.01 |
| Sucralose (half free, half encapsulated) | Sweetness boost; encapsulated part makes flavor last longer | 0.2 | 0.005 |
| **Total** | | **100.0** | **2.70** |

**Optional premium upgrade:** add **1–2% hydroxyapatite** (the mineral tooth
enamel is made of) and cut sorbitol by the same amount. It gives you a strong
story ("coats teeth in the mineral they're made of") at a higher ingredient cost.

**Rough nutrition per stick** (the co-packer calculates the real numbers):
about 4–5 calories, 2 g total carbohydrate, 0 g sugar, ~1.6 g sugar alcohols.
That's close to the placeholder panel on your mockup.

## 4. Flavor variations

Start from the Peppermint base and swap only the parts below.

### Blue Raspberry

| Change | Amount | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Swap peppermint for blue-raspberry flavor | 2.5–3.0% | |
| Add malic acid | **≤ 0.3%** | A little tang. Keep it low: acid fights the dental message. Ask the co-packer for a **"low-acid / buffered"** fruit profile. |
| Color | None | The gum stays white; the flavor color goes on the box. A gum sold on *whitening* shouldn't turn your tongue blue. |

### Pickle

| Change | Amount | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Swap peppermint for dill-pickle flavor (dill, a little garlic note optional) | 2.5% | |
| Add salt | ~0.3% | Real pickle taste |
| Add sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) | ~1.0% | Salty-friendly, neutralizes acid, adds extra polish |
| Sour note | From flavor, **not** vinegar/acid | Same dental reason as above |
| Color | None | Natural green options (like turmeric) stain teeth |

Pickle is your **attention-getter**. People will film themselves trying it.
Peppermint is what most customers will buy again. Launch Peppermint first, or all
three with Peppermint as the biggest order.

## 5. What to ask a co-packer

Search for **"private label sugar-free gum manufacturer"** or
**"xylitol gum co-packer"**. Ask every candidate these questions:

1. **Minimum order quantity (MOQ)?** Gum MOQs are often tens of thousands of
   cartons. Some offer smaller "pilot runs." Ask.
2. Do you have an **existing xylitol stick-gum base** we can start from?
   Adapting a stock recipe costs far less than developing one from scratch.
3. Can you hit **≥ 1 g xylitol per stick** with xylitol listed first?
4. Do you have **whitening data** (stain-removal lab tests) for any base? You
   need this to print "whitening."
5. Can you print **custom stick wrappers with a different message on each
   stick**, inside and out, with a print repeat of 15? Which food-safe inks?
6. Can you pack into **my carton design** (3.00 × 2.40 × 0.35 in, 15 sticks)?
7. Food-safety certification? Look for **SQF, BRCGS or FSSC 22000**.
8. Shelf life? Gum is typically 12–24 months.
9. Cost per carton at 10k, 25k and 50k units?

## 6. Wrapper printing notes

- Wrappers print on a continuous roll. "A different message on every stick"
  comes from a **print repeat**: a set of 15 messages that repeats down the
  roll, so each carton gets all 15. Your list is in `wrapper-messages.md`.
- Keep inside text **≤ 120 characters** so it's readable on a stick wrapper.
- The kids' origami idea: a stick wrapper is too small for instructions, so
  print them **inside the carton** or on an insert card.
