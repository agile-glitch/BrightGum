# bright gum: long-lasting xylitol formula sheets (alternative)

> **Alternative version.** bright's main recipes have no xylitol and are in
> [`formula-sheets.md`](formula-sheets.md); they're built long-lasting too.

A separate set of recipes built for **flavor that lasts longer**. The standard
xylitol recipes are in [`formula-sheets-xylitol.md`](formula-sheets-xylitol.md). Send a manufacturer both and let a side-by-side chew test decide.

> **Status: untested.** These are development formulas on paper. "Long-lasting"
> is the design goal, not a measured result. The timed chew test at the end of
> this sheet is how you prove it.

## What makes these last longer

Flavor in gum seems to fade when the sweetness fades, and xylitol dissolves in
the first few minutes. So the long-lasting versions release both flavor and
sweetness in two stages.

| Change from the standard recipe | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| **Flavor split in two:** liquid flavor for the first burst, plus encapsulated flavor (spray-dried onto gum arabic) that releases as you chew | Liquid flavor washes out in minutes; the encapsulated part keeps arriving |
| **Slow-release sweeteners:** encapsulated sucralose and encapsulated acesulfame potassium, about three times the slow-release sweetener of the standard recipe | Sweetness carries on after the xylitol is gone, so the flavor still registers |
| **Long-flavor-release gum base** | Gum bases differ a lot in how tightly they hold flavor |
| **More encapsulated flavor in the fruit recipes** than in the mints | Fruit flavors fade fastest |
| **Encapsulated menthol in the mints** | Cooling keeps the gum feeling fresh after the mint oil fades |
| **Foil protection:** foil-lined wrappers, or plain paper wrappers inside a sealed foil-lined pack (see "Wrapper options") | Unprotected paper lets flavor escape in storage and lets the gum dry out |

What stays the same: **1.08 g xylitol per stick as the first ingredient**, the
whitening system, no colors, no major allergens, 2.70 g sticks.

**About the encapsulated ingredients.** They're bought ready-made from flavor
and sweetener suppliers. The percentages below are "as supplied," meaning the
weight of the coated ingredient including its coating:

- *Encapsulated flavor* is about 20% flavor on 80% gum arabic.
- *Encapsulated sweeteners* are about 30% sweetener inside a coating. Ask for a
  coating that counts as gum base, so it adds no extra line to the label. The
  manufacturer confirms how it's listed.

## Wrapper options

Two ways to wrap the sticks. Get a quote for both; the right choice depends on
the price difference at your order size.

| | Option A: foil-lined paper on every stick | Option B: plain paper, message printed inside |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Each stick wrapped in a foil/paper laminate, message printed on the paper side | Each stick wrapped in plain (blank outside) paper with the message printed on the inside. One sealed foil-lined inner pack or clear overwrap goes around all 15 sticks to keep them fresh. |
| Freshness and flavor | Best: every stick is protected until it's unwrapped | Good while the pack is sealed; sticks lose flavor faster once it's opened |
| Cost | Material is cheap, about 1 to 3 cents a carton on supplier listings. The hurdles are a large minimum order (300 to 500 kg, enough for about 100,000 cartons) and printing plates at $200 to $800 per color. | Likely a cent or two less per carton, with much smaller minimum orders. One piece of foil or film per carton instead of fifteen. |
| Many different messages | Each new set of messages needs new printing plates | Cheap to change and expand the message set, especially with digital printing (no plates) |
| The reveal | Message is visible on the paper side | Message is hidden until the stick is unwrapped, which suits the commercials |

**Suggested approach:** start with **Option B** for the first runs. Its small
minimum orders and plate-free printing suit a launch with many different
messages, and most stick gum on shelves is already packed this way
(paper-wrapped sticks inside a sealed pack). Cost figures are from
`finance/bright-gum-cost-study.pdf`.
Move to Option A later if chew tests or customer feedback show the flavor fading
after the pack is opened.

Either way, the inside print touches the gum, so it needs **food-safe,
low-migration inks**. The wrapper printer will specify these.

## Standard vs. long-lasting at a glance

| | Standard (`formula-sheets-xylitol.md`) | Long-lasting (this sheet) |
|---|---|---|
| Flavor | All liquid, 2.5–2.8% | Liquid 1.8–2.0% + encapsulated 1.5–2.0% |
| Slow-release sweetener (as supplied) | About 0.3% | 0.8–1.0% |
| Sweeteners on the label | Sucralose | Sucralose, acesulfame potassium |
| Extra label ingredients | None | Gum arabic, acesulfame potassium |
| Gum base | Standard sugar-free | Long-flavor-release grade |
| Expected flavor curve | Strong start, fades early (fruit fastest) | Slightly softer start, holds longer |
| Ingredient cost | Lower | Higher: encapsulated ingredients cost more. Get quotes for both. |

## Nutrition

The label values work out the same as the standard recipes: **0 calories**
(about 4.1–4.2 calculated), **2 g total carbohydrate**, **0 g sugars**, sodium
**15 mg** (Pickle **25 mg**) and calcium **40 mg (3% DV)**.

One number to watch: sugar alcohols calculate to about **1.5 g** per stick, right
on the line between rounding to 1 g and 2 g. The lab analysis decides which goes
on the label.

## Trade-offs to decide

1. **Two more ingredients on the label.** Gum arabic is a natural tree gum.
   Acesulfame potassium is an artificial sweetener, like the sucralose already
   in the recipe. If you'd rather keep sucralose as the only sweetener, ask the
   manufacturer to replace the encapsulated acesulfame potassium with more
   encapsulated sucralose.
2. **Cost.** Expect a higher ingredient cost per carton. Ask for quotes on both
   versions before choosing.
3. **The cartons show the standard ingredient lists.** If you choose a
   long-lasting recipe, the ingredient list on that box changes to the one in
   its sheet above.

## Timed chew test (how to prove "long-lasting")

Ask the manufacturer to run this on pilot samples, or run it yourself on campus.

- **Who:** 10–15 people.
- **Samples:** the standard recipe, the long-lasting recipe and one leading
  store-bought gum of the same flavor, unlabeled.
- **How:** chew one stick. Rate **flavor strength** and **sweetness** from 0 to
  10 at 30 seconds, then at 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15 and 20 minutes.
- **Targets (suggested):**
  - Mints: flavor still 5/10 or higher at 10 minutes.
  - Fruit and Pickle: flavor still 4/10 or higher at 8 minutes.
  - Every flavor: at least as long as the store-bought gum.
- **Also check:** the first-minute flavor isn't weaker than the standard recipe
  by more than 1 point.

Until this test is done, don't print "long-lasting" on a box or use it in ads.
Like "whitening," it's a claim you need evidence for.
