# bright gum: food regulations checklist (US)

This is what applies to selling a sugar-free xylitol gum in the United States.
It's organized as a checklist so you can tick items off.

> **I'm not a lawyer or a regulatory consultant.** This is a map of what to
> look into, with the actual rules cited. Before you print production
> packaging, have your co-packer's regulatory team or a food-labeling
> consultant review the final label. That review costs far less than a reprint
> or a recall.

**The two agencies that matter most:**

- **FDA (Food and Drug Administration)**: what's in the gum and what the label says.
- **FTC (Federal Trade Commission)**: what your ads, website and social posts claim.

### Current mockup choices (packaging/bright-cartons.pdf)

The mockup cartons deliberately leave off "Does not promote tooth decay," the dog
warning, the laxative statement and the "Distributed by" line, to keep them
clean. The first three are optional. **The "Distributed by" name and address is
required** (21 CFR 101.5) and must be on every production carton.

## 2. Ingredients must be approved for food

Your co-packer will handle most of this, but you should know what to ask for.

- [ ] **Gum base** must be made of substances listed in **21 CFR 172.615**
      (chewing gum base).
- [ ] **Xylitol** is an approved food additive (21 CFR 172.395).
- [ ] **Sodium tripolyphosphate** (21 CFR 182.1810) and **calcium carbonate**
      (21 CFR 184.1191) are GRAS ("generally recognized as safe").
- [ ] **Colors** must be FDA-listed (21 CFR Parts 73/74). Spirulina extract is
      listed for gum.
- [ ] Ask for a **Certificate of Analysis (COA)** for each batch, including
      **heavy metals**. Calcium carbonate can carry trace **lead**, which matters
      for California (see section 6).

## 3. Label requirements (21 CFR Part 101)

Every carton needs:

- [ ] **Statement of identity** on the front: e.g. *"Peppermint Sugar-Free Gum."*
- [ ] **Net quantity** on the bottom 30% of the front: *"15 Sticks / Net Wt 1.4 oz (40.5 g)."*
      (Use the real weight from the co-packer.)
- [ ] **Ingredient list** in descending order by weight.
- [ ] **Allergen statement** if any major allergen is present (FALCPA; sesame added by the FASTER Act). The v1 formula has none, but confirm the gum base and flavors with the supplier.
- [ ] **Name and place of business** of the manufacturer, packer or distributor.
- [ ] **Nutrition Facts.** On a small carton you can use the **tabular or linear
      (side-by-side) format** to save space (21 CFR 101.9(j)(13)). Your
      label consultant will choose the format.
- [ ] **Country of origin** if the gum is made outside the US (Customs rule).

## 4. Claims: what each one requires

| Claim | Rule | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **"Sugar-free"** | 21 CFR 101.60(c) | Less than 0.5 g sugar per serving. Some sugar-free foods must also say "not a reduced calorie food" or "not for weight control" next to the claim. **Ask your label reviewer** whether that applies to your final numbers. |
| **"Does not promote tooth decay"** | 21 CFR 101.80 (dental caries health claim) | Must be sugar-free, and the sweetener must be an eligible sugar alcohol (xylitol and sorbitol both qualify). The full claim wording is longer; small packages may use the short version. |
| **"Whitening"** / "removes surface stains" | FTC Act, Section 5 | You need test data on *your* formula. Keep files of it. |
| **"Anti-cavity," "prevents cavities," "fights plaque," "strengthens enamel"** | Drug claims | ❌ Don't use these on gum, the website or in ads. |
| **"Natural"** | FDA policy (informal) | Avoid it unless every ingredient is natural. With sucralose and gum base, it isn't. |
| **"Dentist recommended"** | FTC | Only with an actual survey of dentists. The **ADA Seal** is a real program you could apply for later; it requires clinical evidence. |

**Ads count too.** Everything above also applies to TikToks, influencer posts and
your website. Influencers you pay or send free product must disclose it
(**#ad** or **"paid partnership"**) under the FTC Endorsement Guides.

## 5. Recommended warnings (not legally required, but standard)

- [ ] **"Keep away from dogs. Xylitol is toxic to dogs."** It builds trust, and
      vets will notice.
- [ ] **"Excess consumption may have a laxative effect."** It's required for
      sorbitol only at much higher levels (21 CFR 101.17), but standard on
      sugar-alcohol gum.
- [ ] **"Not recommended for children under 3"** (common on gum because of choking).

## 6. California Proposition 65

If you sell in California (including online orders shipped there), products with
listed chemicals above "safe harbor" levels need a Prop 65 warning. **Lead** in
calcium carbonate is the common trap for gum and mints.

- [ ] Ask the co-packer for heavy-metal test results on the finished gum.
- [ ] If lead is near the limit, switch to a purer calcium carbonate grade or a
      different polish.

## 7. Food safety & business setup

- [ ] **Form an LLC** before you sign contracts.
- [ ] **Co-packer** must be FDA-registered and follow FSMA (the Food Safety
      Modernization Act) preventive-controls rules. Ask for their FDA
      registration and their **SQF/BRCGS audit certificate**.
- [ ] As the brand owner who doesn't make or store the food, you generally
      **don't need your own FDA facility registration**. Any warehouse that
      stores your product does.
- [ ] **Product liability insurance**, typically $1–2M per occurrence. Most
      retailers and co-packers require it.
- [ ] **Supply agreement** with the co-packer covering recipe ownership,
      quality specs and who pays if a batch is bad.
- [ ] **Recall plan**: lot codes printed on every carton (the co-packer adds
      these) and a written plan for pulling product.

## 8. Intellectual property

- [ ] **Trademark search** at USPTO (tmsearch.uspto.gov) for "bright" in
      **Class 30** (confectionery, gum) and **Class 3/5** (oral care). "Bright"
      alone is a common word, so file **"bright gum"** plus the **smile logo** as
      a design mark.
- [ ] Use **™** now. Switch to **®** only after registration.
- [ ] Bible verses: use the **KJV** (public domain). Printing NIV, ESV or NLT on
      a commercial product usually needs the publisher's permission.

## 9. Selling outside the US later

Other countries have different rules. In the **EU**, for example, xylitol gum
has its own approved health claims with specific wording and conditions, and
color rules differ. Treat each new country as a new label review.
